## The Mathematics of Capital Longevity: Withdrawal Rate vs. Compounding Velocity

The survival of a retirement corpus depends on the balance between your **Monthly Withdrawal Rate ($W$)**, your **Portfolio Growth Rate ($r$)**, and your **Annual Inflation Hike ($s$)**:

$$ B_{m} = B_{m-1} \times (1 + r_m) - W_m $$

Where:
- **$B_m$** = Portfolio balance at the end of month $m$
- **$r_m$** = Monthly portfolio compounding return ($\text{Annual Return} \div 12$)
- **$W_m$** = Monthly cash payout withdrawn at month $m$

### The Golden Rule of Perpetual SWP
If your annualized withdrawal rate is equal to or less than your portfolio's net compounding return ($W_{\text{annual}} \le r$), **your principal corpus never depletes**. At an 8% expected portfolio return on ₹50 Lakh:
- $8\% \times \text{₹50,00,000} = \text{₹4,00,000 / year} = \text{₹33,333 / month}$.
- Withdrawing ₹30,000 to ₹33,333 per month allows you to withdraw cash indefinitely without touching the original ₹50 Lakh principal!

## 5. Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I get ₹50,000 per month from a ₹50 Lakh SWP?
Withdrawing ₹50,000 per month represents an unsustainable **12.0% annual withdrawal rate**. At that pace, even with an 8% portfolio return, the entire ₹50 Lakh corpus will be completely depleted in approximately **13 years**. For a sustainable pension, keep withdrawals to ₹30,000 to ₹33,000/month, or explore our [₹1 Crore SWP Calculator](/1-crore-swp-calculator) to support higher lifestyle expenses.

### How does TDS apply to mutual fund SWP payouts?
For resident Indian individuals, mutual funds do **not** deduct TDS on capital gains from SWP redemptions (unlike Bank FDs where TDS is deducted under Section 194A at 10%). You report your capital gains at the end of the financial year and pay applicable Section 112A taxes via advance tax installments.
