> **Direct Answer (BLUF):** Under **Finance (No. 2) Act 2024 (Section 112A)**, long-term capital gains (>12 months) on Indian equity mutual funds and listed stocks are taxed at **12.5% base rate (13.0% effective with 4% Health & Education Cess)** on profits exceeding **₹1,25,000 per financial year**. On a **₹25 Lakh portfolio** (cost ₹15 Lakhs) compounding at **12% for 10 years**, holding untouched triggers **₹8.00 Lakhs** in LTCG tax at exit, whereas systematically harvesting **₹1.25 Lakhs/year** cuts exit tax to **₹6.54 Lakhs**—saving **₹1.46 Lakhs** legally. Splitting a current redemption across **March 31 and April 1** immediately captures **₹2.50 Lakhs** (`2 × ₹1.25L`) tax-free.

### How Section 112A Tax Harvesting Works in India

Every financial year (April 1 to March 31), the Indian Income Tax Act grants individual and HUF investors a **use-it-or-lose-it ₹1,25,000 tax-free LTCG exemption**. If you do not book at least ₹1.25 Lakh of long-term gains before March 31, that annual exemption lapses permanently.

1. **The March 31 + April 1 Split Strategy:** If you need to withdraw a large lump sum (or initiate an SWP corpus rebalance) near the end of the financial year, redeeming half on **March 30–31** and half on **April 1–2** utilizes two distinct financial-year limits (`₹1.25L + ₹1.25L = ₹2.50L` tax-free), saving up to **₹16,250** in instant tax!
2. **No Wash-Sale Rule in India:** Unlike the United States IRS wash-sale restriction, Indian tax law permits redeeming mutual fund units to book ₹1.25L tax-free gain and immediately repurchasing the same scheme (or switching to a direct plan) to step up your cost basis.
