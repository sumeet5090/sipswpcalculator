### Key Statutory NPS Rules Every Indian Investor Must Know

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                   NPS TIER-1 LIFECYCLE AT A GLANCE                     │
└───────────────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────────────┘
                                    │
    ┌───────────────────────────────┴───────────────────────────────┐
    ▼                                                               ▼
Accumulation Phase (Age 18 to 60)               Exit & Maturity (Age 60 / Superannuation)
• Minimum ₹500/year (Tier-1)                    • Max 60% Lump Sum (100% Tax-Free Sec 10(12A))
• Sec 80CCD(1B): Extra ₹50,000 deduction        • Min 40% Annuity (Lifelong monthly pension)
• Sec 80CCD(2): Up to 10% employer match        • Exception: 100% Lump Sum if corpus ≤ ₹5 Lakh
```

1. **Mandatory 40% Annuity Purchase:** Under PFRDA exit regulations, upon reaching age 60, you must allocate at least **40% of your accumulated corpus** to purchase a life annuity from an IRDAI-registered Annuity Service Provider (ASP) such as LIC, SBI Life, or HDFC Life.
2. **60% Tax-Free Lump Sum (Section 10(12A)):** Up to **60% of your corpus** can be withdrawn as a lump sum upon superannuation. Under Section 10(12A) of the Income Tax Act, this entire 60% lump sum is **100% exempt from income tax**.
3. **Small Corpus Exemption (≤ ₹5 Lakh):** If your total accumulated NPS corpus at age 60 is **₹5,00,000 or less**, PFRDA regulations permit you to withdraw **100% of the corpus as a tax-free lump sum** without any mandatory annuity purchase.
4. **Annuity Pension Taxation:** While purchasing the annuity is tax-free, the recurring monthly pension payments received from the annuity provider are treated as income and taxed at your marginal income tax slab rate in the year of receipt.

### Tax Deduction Benefits Across Regimes

NPS offers a unique triple-window tax deduction structure in India:

| Section | Description | Old Tax Regime | New Tax Regime |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Section 80CCD(1)** | Employee / Self-contribution up to 10% of Basic + DA (or 20% of gross income for self-employed) | Eligible (within overall ₹1.5L Section 80C limit) | Not Available |
| **Section 80CCD(1B)** | Exclusive additional voluntary deduction for NPS Tier-1 | **Up to ₹50,000** (above ₹1.5L 80C limit) | Not Available |
| **Section 80CCD(2)** | Employer's contribution up to 10% of Basic + DA (14% for Central/State Govt) | **Eligible** (no upper monetary limit; subject to ₹7.5L overall employer PF+NPS cap) | **Eligible** (retained under New Tax Regime) |

> [!TIP]
> Under the **New Tax Regime**, **Section 80CCD(2)** (employer contribution up to 10% of salary) remains one of the very few salary-structuring deductions permitted by the Income Tax Department. High earners can significantly lower their tax liability by asking their employers to route up to 10% of Basic pay into NPS.

### NPS Asset Allocation Options: Active Choice vs Auto Choice

PFRDA allows subscribers to choose how their monthly contributions are allocated across four asset classes:
- **Asset Class E (Equity):** High-growth equity instruments (up to 75% under Active Choice until age 50, then tapering down).
- **Asset Class C (Corporate Debt):** Fixed-income instruments issued by corporate entities and PSUs.
- **Asset Class G (Government Securities):** Central and State government bonds offering sovereign safety.
- **Asset Class A (Alternative Assets):** Real estate investment trusts (REITs), InvITs, and venture capital (capped at 5%).

Subscribers choosing **Auto Choice (Lifecycle Funds)** have their equity allocation automatically de-risked and shifted towards debt as they grow older:
- **Aggressive Lifecycle Fund (LC-75):** Begins with 75% equity until age 35, gradually reducing to 15% by age 55.
- **Moderate Lifecycle Fund (LC-50 - Default):** Begins with 50% equity until age 35, tapering to 10% by age 55.
- **Conservative Lifecycle Fund (LC-25):** Begins with 25% equity until age 35, tapering to 5% by age 55.

### Frequently Asked Questions

#### Is the 60% NPS lump-sum withdrawal tax-free at retirement?
Yes, under Section 10(12A) of the Income Tax Act, 100% of the 60% lump-sum withdrawal from NPS Tier-1 upon superannuation (age 60) is completely tax-free. The remaining 40% must be used to purchase an annuity, which is exempt at purchase, though the periodic monthly pension received from the annuity is taxable at your applicable income slab rate.

#### What is the mandatory annuity purchase rule under NPS at age 60?
Under PFRDA exit regulations, an NPS Tier-1 subscriber must allocate at least 40% of their accumulated retirement corpus to purchase a life annuity from an IRDAI-registered Annuity Service Provider (ASP). However, if the total accumulated corpus at retirement is ₹5,00,000 (₹5 Lakhs) or less, the subscriber has the option to withdraw 100% of the corpus as a lump sum without purchasing an annuity.

#### What tax deductions can I claim for NPS contributions in India?
NPS offers three distinct tax deduction windows: Section 80CCD(1) up to 10% of salary within the ₹1.5L 80C limit (Old Regime), Section 80CCD(1B) up to an exclusive ₹50,000 above 80C (Old Regime), and Section 80CCD(2) employer contributions up to 10% of Basic + DA, which is available under both the Old and New Tax Regimes.
