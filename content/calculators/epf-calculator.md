### How EPFO Interest & Employer Contributions Actually Work

Unlike standard mutual fund SIPs that compound continuously on daily NAVs, the **Employees' Provident Fund Organisation (EPFO)** uses a specialized statutory ledger mechanic:

1. **Monthly Running-Balance Accrual:** Every month, interest is calculated on your closing running balance at $r_m = \frac{8.25\%}{12}$.
2. **Annual Financial-Year Crediting:** That accumulated monthly interest is credited to your EPF passbook **once per year on March 31**, meaning intra-year interest does not earn interest until the new financial year begins.
3. **12% Employee + 12% Employer Split:**
   - **Employee Share (12% of Basic + DA):** Goes 100% into your EPF account (eligible for Section 80C deduction under Old Regime).
   - **Employer Share (12% of Basic + DA):** Split between **EPS (Employees' Pension Scheme)** at **8.33%** and **Employer EPF** at **3.67%**.
   - **₹15,000 Wage Ceiling Rule:** Under statutory EPFO rules, the 8.33% EPS diversion is **capped at ₹1,250/month** ($8.33\%$ of ₹15,000). Any remaining employer contribution above ₹1,250 automatically flows into your interest-bearing EPF corpus!

### Finance Act 2021: The ₹2.5 Lakh Tax-Free Interest Threshold

Under Section 10(11) and 10(12) of the Income Tax Act:
- **Up to ₹2,50,000/year** in combined **Employee EPF + VPF** contributions earns **100% tax-free interest (EEE status)**.
- If your combined Employee EPF + VPF exceeds ₹2,50,000 in a financial year, the interest earned strictly on the *excess contribution* above ₹2.5 Lakhs is taxed at your marginal income tax slab rate.
- **Important Note:** Your **Employer's EPF contribution** does *not* count toward the ₹2.5 Lakh employee threshold (it is separately subject to the aggregate ₹7.5 Lakh cap across PF, NPS, and superannuation).
