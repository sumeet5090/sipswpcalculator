## The Mathematics of Reaching ₹2 Crores: Compounding Mechanics

To calculate the required monthly installment ($P$) to achieve a future value ($FV = \text{₹2,00,00,000}$), financial engineers solve the standard future value annuity equation via inverse iteration:

$$ FV = P \times \left[ \frac{(1 + r)^n - 1}{r} \right] \times (1 + r) $$

Where:
- **$FV$** = Target Corpus ($\text{₹2,00,00,000}$)
- **$P$** = Monthly SIP installment
- **$r$** = Monthly expected rate of return ($\text{Annual CAGR} \div 12$)
- **$n$** = Total number of compounding months ($\text{Years} \times 12$)

When annual step-up compounding is enabled, each 12-month block escalates by your chosen increment percentage ($s$, typically 10%), transforming the cash-flow series into an expanding arithmetic-geometric progression that significantly reduces starting capital requirements.

## 4. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Can I reach ₹2 Crores in 10 years with a ₹50,000 monthly SIP?
Yes. At a 12% equity CAGR with a **10% annual step-up**, starting at ₹50,000/month accumulates **₹1.86 Crores** in 10 years. Increasing your starting commitment to **₹59,271/month** crosses the ₹2 Crore mark exactly in Year 10.

### What mutual fund asset allocation is recommended for a ₹2 Crore goal?
For a 10 to 15-year horizon, financial planners recommend a disciplined equity portfolio:
- **Large-Cap / Nifty 50 Index Fund:** 45% (stable foundation)
- **Flexi-Cap / Multi-Cap Fund:** 35% (dynamic market capitalization exposure)
- **Mid-Cap / Small-Cap Fund:** 20% (alpha generation and compounding kicker)

### How is the ₹2 Crore corpus taxed upon withdrawal?
Equity mutual fund holdings held for longer than 12 months qualify as Long-Term Capital Assets under **Section 112A**. Gains up to ₹1.25 Lakh per financial year are completely tax-exempt. Gains exceeding ₹1.25 Lakh are taxed at **12.5% base rate (13.0% effective inclusive of 4% health & education cess)**. Staggering redemptions via an SWP spreads gains across multiple financial years, maximizing tax exemptions.
