## 1. The Statutory Gratuity Formula (Covered vs. Non-Covered)

The statutory formula depends on whether your organization is covered under the Payment of Gratuity Act:

### A. Employees Covered under the Act (Most Private & Public Sector Companies)
Any factory, mine, oilfield, plantation, port, railway company, shop, or commercial establishment employing **10 or more individuals** on any day of the preceding 12 months is legally covered under the Act.

$$ \text{Gross Gratuity} = \frac{15 \times \text{Last Drawn Monthly Salary} \times \text{Effective Tenure (Years)}}{26} $$

Where:
- **Last Drawn Monthly Salary:** Basic Pay + Dearness Allowance (DA). HRA, conveyance, performance bonuses, and special allowances are strictly excluded.
- **26 Working Days:** The Act defines a working month as 26 days (30 calendar days minus 4 weekly Sundays).
- **15 Days Wages:** 15 days of salary per completed year of service ($\frac{15}{26} \approx 57.69\%$ of monthly pay per year served).
- **The 6-Month Rounding Rule:** If service in your final year exceeds 6 months ($> 6\text{ months}$), the tenure is rounded **UP** to the next full integer year. (For example, 7 years 7 months rounds up to 8 years; 7 years 5 months rounds down to 7 years).

### B. Employees Not Covered under the Act
For establishments with fewer than 10 employees not covered under the statute:

$$ \text{Gross Gratuity} = \frac{15 \times \text{Average Monthly Salary (Last 10 Months)} \times \text{Completed Years of Service}}{30} $$

Where a month is counted as 30 days, and only completed full years are counted without fractional rounding.

## 3. Taxation Rules under Section 10(10) of the Income Tax Act

How your gratuity payout is taxed depends strictly on your employment category:

1. **Government Employees (Central & State Govt, Defense, Local Authorities):**
   - Gratuity received on retirement, superannuation, or resignation is **100% exempt from income tax without any monetary upper limit**.
2. **Non-Government Salaried Employees Covered under the Act:**
   - Under Section 10(10)(ii), the least of the following three amounts is completely exempt from income tax:
     1. Actual gratuity amount received from the employer.
     2. Statutory maximum ceiling: **₹20,00,000 (₹20 Lakhs)**.
     3. Statutory formula amount: $\frac{15}{26} \times \text{Last Drawn Salary} \times \text{Tenure}$.
   - Any gratuity received in excess of ₹20,00,000 is added to your taxable income under the head *"Income from Salaries"* and taxed at your marginal slab rate.
3. **Non-Government Employees Not Covered under the Act:**
   - Tax-exempt up to the least of: actual gratuity, ₹20 Lakhs statutory limit, or $\frac{\text{Half Month's Average Salary}}{30} \times \text{Completed Years}$.

## 4. The 5-Year Vesting Cliff & Important Legal Exceptions

Under Section 4(1) of the Act, gratuity is payable to an employee upon separation only if they have rendered **continuous service for not less than 5 years**:
- **On Resignation:** If you resign after 4 years and 5 months, you are legally **ineligible** for gratuity.
- **The 4 Years 240 Days Judicial Rule:** Multiple High Courts and the Supreme Court of India have ruled that completing **4 years and 240 days** (in a 6-day work week establishment) or **4 years and 190 days** (in a 5-day work week establishment) in the 5th year fulfills the continuous service condition, granting legal entitlement.
- **Exceptions to the 5-Year Rule:** The mandatory 5-year service requirement is completely **waived** if separation occurs due to:
  1. Death of the employee (gratuity is paid in full to the legal heir/nominee).
  2. Permanent disablement caused by accident or disease rendering the employee incapable of service.

## 6. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Can an employer pay more than ₹20 Lakhs gratuity?
Yes. The Payment of Gratuity Act specifies the statutory minimum. Employers are free to pay higher gratuity under contract or corporate policy. However, any amount received above ₹20 Lakhs is subject to income tax at your standard slab rate.

### Is gratuity deducted from my monthly salary?
No. Gratuity is 100% funded by the employer. Some companies list gratuity as a line item inside your Cost-to-Company (CTC) package (~4.81% of Basic Pay), but it is never deducted from your monthly gross pay.

### How does the 6-month rounding rule work in practice?
If you serve 8 years and 7 months, your employer must calculate gratuity for **9 years**. If you serve 8 years and 6 months or less, it is calculated for **8 years**.
