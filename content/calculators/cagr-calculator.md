## What is CAGR (Compound Annual Growth Rate)?

**Compound Annual Growth Rate (CAGR)** is the annualized mean growth rate of an investment over a specified period longer than one year. It represents the smooth annual rate of return that would be required for an investment to grow from its beginning balance to its ending balance, assuming the profits were reinvested at the end of each period.

Unlike simple average returns—which obscure the impact of market volatility—CAGR provides an accurate, apples-to-apples metric to compare mutual funds, equity portfolios, gold, and real estate.

### The CAGR Mathematical Formula

CAGR is calculated as:

$$\mathbf{\text{CAGR} = \left(\frac{V_{\text{final}}}{V_{\text{begin}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{t}} - 1}$$

*   **$V_{\text{final}}$** = Final investment value (current portfolio balance or redemption value)
*   **$V_{\text{begin}}$** = Initial investment amount (starting capital)
*   **$t$** = Investment tenure in years (supports fractional years such as $3.5$ years)

## CAGR vs. Absolute Return vs. XIRR

Understanding the difference between return metrics prevents common investment mistakes:

| Metric | Best Used For | Accounts for Time? | Handles Irregular Cashflows? |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Absolute Return** | Single lumpsum investments under 1 year | ❌ No | ❌ No |
| **CAGR** | Single lumpsum investments over 1+ years | ✅ Yes | ❌ No (Single point-to-point only) |
| **XIRR** | Systematic Investment Plans (SIP), multi-date deposits/SWP | ✅ Yes | ✅ Yes (Excel-standard IRR) |

### Why Absolute Return Can Be Misleading

If an investment delivers a **$100\%$ absolute return**, it sounds extraordinary. However:
* If it took **$1$ year**, your CAGR is **$100\%$** (phenomenal).
* If it took **$5$ years**, your CAGR is **$14.87\%$** (strong equity return).
* If it took **$15$ years**, your CAGR is **$4.73\%$** (fails to beat Indian inflation).

Without calculating CAGR, you cannot assess whether an asset beat the risk-free rate of return.
